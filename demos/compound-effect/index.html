Sources & further reading

  • The Compound Effect, by Darren Hardy. Source of the four-dollar-a-day coffee example and the page's central theme: small choices, repeated consistently over time, can produce large results.
  • The Latte Factor & The Automatic Millionaire, by David Bach. The daily-spending and automatic-saving framework behind the coffee examples and the $5-versus-$10-a-day comparison in section II.
  • Present Value on Math is Fun. The worked $1,100 / $500 / $900 discounting examples in section IV.
  • The Shockingly Simple Math Behind Early Retirement, by Mr. Money Mustache. Further reading on how savings rate and spending level shape the time required to reach financial independence.
  • The latte-factor debate: the structural critique and Chicago Booth Review on the behavioral case. Context for section IX's conclusion that large financial decisions and recurring habits can both matter.
  • J.P. Morgan Asset Management's 1999–2018 stay-invested analysis. The section X defaults reproduce its S&P 500 comparison: 5.6% annualized when fully invested versus 2.0% after removing the ten best days. The current Guide to Retirement rolls the period forward, so its figures differ.
  • Calculator references for cross-checking scenarios: Financial Mentor, Calculator.net, Nesteggly's retirement calculator, and the SEC compound interest calculator.

Assumptions: returns are nominal and constant; contributions are made at the end of each period; habit frequencies are editable where shown, otherwise annualized × 365; taxes are ignored except where shown; the currency selector changes symbols only, not amounts.
